Backtest

A systematic market-neutral arbitrage strategy that trades a short-lived, recurring dislocation in a liquid international equity universe. Backtested over its full available history, net of modeled trading costs and two risk overlays described below.

This is original research, not a published strategy. This page shows performance only. Beta and correlation

Beta against six benchmarks spanning both trading sessions, with 95% confidence intervals, shown against the exposure a fully invested book would carry. Largest beta 0.032, largest correlation 0.062, and no benchmark explains more than 0.38% of the variance.

Cost scenario: 10bps per round trip

Every other figure on this page is priced at the broker's published retail schedule: about 62bps for a round trip once taxes, commissions, spreads and slippage are counted. This card re-runs the same $100,000 book with every round trip, both legs, entry and exit, charged a flat 10bps all-in instead (about 2.5bps per order), a cost level a market-making desk could plausibly reach. Signals, holding rule and volume limits are unchanged; the entry rule scales with cost, so at this level it also admits more trades.

Capacity

A small-cap book by design. It only trades when a name actually dislocates, so some days it holds a handful of positions and some days none at all. This puts a limit on how much capital can be implemented.

No position is allowed to exceed 8% of the traded value of the leg it has to be filled in, measured on the day it trades. Roughly 4% of positions are held below their intended size by that rule, and the figures above are quoted after it.

The ratios therefore fall as size rises. They are computed on the $100,000 the dollar equity curve is drawn at.

Performance shown net of estimated trading costs. Past performance is not indicative of future results. Each day re-trades a flat $100,000; gains and losses are not reinvested.
