Backtest

A systematic market-neutral arbitrage strategy that trades a short-lived, recurring dislocation in a liquid international equity universe. Backtested over its full available history, net of trading costs at a market-maker level of 10bps each way and two risk overlays described below.

This is original research, not a published strategy. This page shows performance only. Drawdown

$100,000 re-traded fresh each day, net of costs at 10bps each way — not compounded, so this is the sum of the strategy's per-trade edge over time, not a claim about reinvested growth. Shown at a scale well within the strategy's own capacity (see below).

Beta and correlation

Beta against six benchmarks spanning both trading sessions, with 95% confidence intervals, shown against the exposure a fully invested book would carry. Largest beta 0.026, largest correlation 0.052, and no benchmark explains more than 0.27% of the variance.

Capacity

A small-cap book by design. It only trades when a name actually dislocates, so some days it holds a handful of positions and some days none at all. This puts a limit on how much capital can be implemented.

No position may exceed 8% of the Hong Kong opening window's traded value, forecast from the previous ten sessions, since the size is fixed at the US close before that window trades. What the open cannot fill is bought back at the next US open; the figures above include it.

The ratios therefore fall as size rises. They are computed on the $100,000 the dollar equity curve is drawn at.
