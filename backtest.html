Backtest

A systematic market-neutral arbitrage strategy that trades a short-lived, recurring dislocation in a liquid international equity universe. Backtested over its full available history, net of trading costs at a market-maker level of 10bps per round trip and two risk overlays described below.

This is original research, not a published strategy. This page shows performance only. The instrument universe, signal thresholds, cost model, and detailed trade logs are my own and aren't disclosed here. Built and backtested independently. Cost basis: 10bps per round trip

Every figure on this page — the equity curve, its statistics and the capacity table — is priced at a flat 10bps for each round trip, both legs, entry and exit (about 2.5bps per order): a cost level a market-making desk could plausibly reach. The paper ledger, and the book as a retail account would trade it, are priced at the broker's published schedule instead: about 62bps for a round trip once taxes, commissions, spreads and slippage are counted. Signals, holding rule and volume limits are the same at both; the entry rule scales with cost, so at 10bps it also admits more trades.

Drawdown

$100,000 re-traded fresh each day, net of trading costs at 10bps per round trip (the cost basis above) — not compounded, so this is the sum of the strategy's per-trade edge over time, not a claim about reinvested growth. Shown at a scale well within the strategy's own capacity (see below).

Beta and correlation

Beta against six benchmarks spanning both trading sessions, with 95% confidence intervals, shown against the exposure a fully invested book would carry. Largest beta 0.032, largest correlation 0.062, and no benchmark explains more than 0.38% of the variance.

Capacity

A small-cap book by design. It only trades when a name actually dislocates, so some days it holds a handful of positions and some days none at all. This puts a limit on how much capital can be implemented.

No position is allowed to exceed 8% of the traded value of the leg it has to be filled in, measured on the day it trades. Roughly 4% of positions are held below their intended size by that rule, and the figures above are quoted after it.

The ratios therefore fall as size rises. They are computed on the $100,000 the dollar equity curve is drawn at.
