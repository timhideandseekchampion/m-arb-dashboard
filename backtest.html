Backtest

A systematic market-neutral arbitrage strategy that trades a short-lived, recurring dislocation in a liquid international equity universe. Backtested over its full available history, net of market-maker costs of 26bps per round trip, with the Hong Kong leg filled in the first 30 seconds of trading rather than at the opening print, and two risk overlays described below.

This is original research, not a published strategy. This page shows performance only. The instrument universe, signal thresholds, cost model, and detailed trade logs are my own and aren't disclosed here. Built and backtested independently. Beta and correlation

Beta against six benchmarks spanning both trading sessions, with 95% confidence intervals, shown against the exposure a fully invested book would carry. Largest beta 0.026, largest correlation 0.052, and no benchmark explains more than 0.27% of the variance.

Capacity

A small-cap book by design. It only trades when a name actually dislocates, so some days it holds a handful of positions and some days none at all. This puts a limit on how much capital can be implemented.

No position may exceed a quarter of what the Hong Kong line trades in the first 30 seconds after the open, forecast from the previous ten sessions, since the size is fixed at the US close. What the open cannot fill is bought back at the next US open; the figures above include it.

The ratios therefore fall as size rises. They are computed on the $100,000 the dollar equity curve is drawn at.
